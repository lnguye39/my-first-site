A book I love

Colorful

by Eto Mori

The story

A soul that has died after committing a terrible wrong is told it has won a lottery: a second chance. To earn its way back into the cycle of rebirth, it must live for a while inside the body of Makoto Kobayashi, a fourteen-year-old boy who has just taken his own life.

Guided by a cheerful, offbeat angel named Purapura, the soul steps into Makoto's world: his family, his school, his secrets. What first seems like a bleak, gray life slowly turns out to be far more complicated, and far more colorful, than it looked from the outside.

Three reasons I recommend it

  1. It handles heavy topics gently

    The book deals with depression, loneliness and suicide, but it does so with warmth and even humor. It never feels preachy, and it leaves you feeling hopeful instead of hopeless.

  2. People are more than one color

    Every character who seems cold or disappointing at first turns out to have their own struggles. It's a powerful reminder that no one is all good or all bad, including yourself.

  3. It's short but stays with you

    You can read it in an afternoon, yet the ending reframes everything that came before it. It's the kind of book you want to hand to a friend the moment you finish.

A favorite quotation

Miserable was having to spend lunch hour by yourself, walk to your next class alone. The sheer fact that there was someone next to me every time I turned to look made me just plain happy. My heart practically exploded. — Eto Mori, Colorful
